# Broken areas

A seed, not a design. Captured 2026-08-13 so the ruling that created it does not
get lost.

## Where it comes from

[excess-fourths](excess-fourths.md) settled that a fourth utterance **saturates
the spot it is spoken in** — no radius, no field, no spread. Fields were
rejected because two overlapping fields would have to be told how to combine and
there is no honest answer to *"what is a frozen rotting swamp"*.

Spots do not overlap, so that question never arises — except in one place. **Two
different fourths saturating the same spot.**

The ruling: they do **not** stack, and they do **not** average. The place
becomes a **broken area** — a third thing, authored by hand, not computed from
its ingredients.

## Why this is the right shape

It converts a composition problem into a content hook. A rule that blends two
world-states is a rule that has to be right for every pair forever; an authored
third thing only has to be good. It is also the same move the rest of the game
already makes — a fourth is hand-authored per word for exactly this reason, and
compound words were shelved for exactly the opposite one (182 bespoke cells was
the wrong shape). **Watch that line.** With seven fourths there are 21 unordered
pairs; with all 30 there are 435. Authoring per *pair* does not scale, so this
almost certainly wants classes of pairing, or a small number of named broken
areas that many pairs route into.

## Open — all of it

- **What is a broken area, in the fiction?** The grammar has been overspoken
  twice in one place. Something about the world stopped agreeing to be a place.
- **Per pair, or per class?** See the arithmetic above. Leaning: a handful of
  named broken areas, with pairs mapped onto them.
- **Is it permanent?** The fourths' own saturation decays at read time from
  `plantedAt`. Does breaking decay, or is it the one thing that does not?
- ⚠️ **It must not be a wall.** `completability` and `no-terminal-states`: a
  broken area may be terrible to fight in, may destroy spoils, may be
  unfarmable — it may not lock content behind itself or strand a player who
  walked in. If a broken area can contain the only route to something, it needs
  an exit that does not require winning.
- **Can it be un-broken?** If not, a player can permanently ruin a spot they
  needed. That is either the best thing about the mechanic or a completability
  violation, depending entirely on the previous bullet.
- **Does it interact with place tags?** Saturation is expected to express itself
  as a derived place tag (region ∪ area ∪ spot union, the way `'dark'` already
  works). A broken area may be a tag too, or may be the thing that suppresses
  the others.
